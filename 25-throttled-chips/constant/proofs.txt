CONSTANT BOUND FOR THE LAG STRATEGY OF solver-a THEOREM 2            (every part marked)

Setting and notation (as in 25-throttled-chips/solver-a/proofs.txt).
  k_x = number of adds at x so far, F_x = firings at x so far, eta_x = chips on x,
  eta_x = k_x - 2F_x + F_{x-1} + F_{x+1}.
  T_x = (3k_x + k_{x-1} + k_{x+1})/5,   lag l_x = T_x - F_x.
  STRATEGY (unchanged from Theorem 2): in step (2), if some ready vertex has lag >= 1, fire a
  ready vertex of largest lag (any tie rule); otherwise pass.
  "After round t" = after step (2) of round t; M_t = the largest lag right after the add of round t.
  For a vertex y:  e_y = e_y(t) = number of the first t adds that are NOT in [y-2, y+2].

Facts taken from Theorem 2 (re-checked here, COMPLETE):
  (a) l_x >= 0 at all times.  (T never decreases; a firing needs lag >= 1 and lowers it by 1.)
  (b) eta_x = eta*_x + 2 l_x - l_{x-1} - l_{x+1},  eta*_x = (k_{x-2}+...+k_{x+2})/5 = (t - e_x)/5.
  (d) If M_t >= 6 then round t fires a vertex whose lag is exactly M_t.
      (A vertex x of largest lag has 2l_x - l_{x-1} - l_{x+1} >= 0, so eta_x >= eta*_x >=
      (k_{x-1}+k_x+k_{x+1})/5 >= T_x/3 >= l_x/3 >= 2: it is ready with lag >= 1, so the rule fires
      a ready vertex of largest lag, and that lag is M_t.)

LEMMA 1 (level sets; COMPLETE).  Fix a real a >= 5 and put Phi_a = sum_z max(l_z - a, 0).
  (i)  In a round with M_t >= a+1:  Phi_a(after round t) <= Phi_a(after round t-1).
  (ii) In a round with M_t <  a+1:  every lag after round t is < a+1.
Proof.  The add raises three lags by 3/5, 1/5, 1/5, so it raises Phi_a by at most 1.
  (i) M_t >= a+1 >= 6, so by (d) a vertex of lag M_t >= a+1 is fired; its lag stays >= a, so
  Phi_a drops by exactly 1.  (ii) a firing only lowers a lag.  QED

LEMMA 2 (local lag bound; COMPLETE).  After every round t, for every vertex y and every real
  a >= 5:        l_y <= a + 7 + e_y(t)/a.
Proof.  Let L = l_y after round t and suppose L > a + 7 (else nothing to prove).
  Let tau <= t be the last round with M_tau < a+1; if there is none put tau = 0 (empty board, all
  lags 0).  Rounds tau+1..t all have M >= a+1, so by Lemma 1(i)
        L - a <= Phi_a(after t) <= Phi_a(after tau).
  After round tau every lag is < a+1 (Lemma 1(ii), or trivially at tau = 0).  Let Z be the set of
  vertices with lag > a after round tau.  Each contributes less than 1 to Phi_a, so
        L - a <= Phi_a(after tau) < |Z|   (in particular Z is not empty).
  Put Z' = Z \ [y-3, y+3].  Then |Z'| >= |Z| - 7 > L - a - 7 > 0.
  For z in Z':  |z - y| >= 4, so z-1, z, z+1 all lie outside [y-2, y+2], and
        a < l_z <= T_z = (3k_z + k_{z-1} + k_{z+1})/5      (values after round tau).
  Sum over z in Z'.  A chip added at x contributes to 5T_z only for z in {x-1, x, x+1}, in total
  at most 3+1+1 = 5, and only chips outside [y-2, y+2] occur.  Hence
        5 a |Z'| < sum_{z in Z'} 5 T_z <= 5 e_y(tau) <= 5 e_y(t).
  So e_y(t) > a |Z'| > a (L - a - 7), i.e. L < a + 7 + e_y(t)/a.  QED

THEOREM A (COMPLETE).  Under the strategy of Theorem 2, against every list, for every vertex y:
     after round t:               eta_y <= t/5 + 34;
     after the add of round t:    eta_y <= t/5 + 35.2.
  So the height never exceeds t/5 + c with c = 35.2, at every step.
Proof.  Lemma 2 with a = 10:  l_y <= 17 + e_y/10 after every round.  By (b) and (a),
     eta_y <= eta*_y + 2 l_y <= (t - e_y)/5 + 34 + e_y/5 = t/5 + 34     after round t.
  Right after the add of round t the lag at y is at most its value after round t-1 plus 3/5, that
  is <= 17 + e_y(t-1)/10 + 3/5 <= 17.6 + e_y(t)/10 (for t = 1 the earlier lag is 0), and (b), (a)
  hold at that moment with the current t and e_y(t):  eta_y <= (t - e_y)/5 + 35.2 + e_y/5.  QED

COROLLARY (COMPLETE).  Every list of length m <= 5h - 177 loses, so
          5h - 176 <= G(h) <= C(h) <= 5h - 6      for all h
  (upper bound: solver-b P3, h >= 2).  G(h) = 5h - O(1) and C(h) = 5h - O(1).
Proof.  For t <= m <= 5h-177: t/5 + 35.2 <= h - 0.2 < h, heights are integers, so every vertex
  holds <= h-1 chips after every step.  QED

REMARKS.
  1. The proof of Lemma 2 never uses that the firing threshold is 1.  For the lazy strategy "fire
     only at lag >= theta" (6 <= theta <= 11) fact (d) holds for M >= theta, Lemma 1 for
     a >= theta - 1, and Theorem A holds verbatim (a = 10).  The tests use theta = 6 and 11, where
     the lemmas are actually exercised (lag reaches 7.2 and 12).
  2. Why this works although the old proof gave sqrt(t): the global potential cannot see WHERE the
     lag is.  Height at y only loses to t/5 by e_y/5 (chips added away from y), and a large lag at
     y needs many other vertices with large lag at one moment, which costs chips away from y.
     The same Lemma 1 gives a global bound max lag < a + t/a (a ~ sqrt t), i.e. the old sqrt(t).
  3. The constant 35.2 is not optimised (the "7" and the use of l_{y+-1} >= 0 are crude).

OBSERVATION B (EVIDENCE ONLY, NOT A PROOF).  The LAG of this strategy is probably NOT bounded by a
  constant (the referee's follow-up 1 asked to prove it is; observed maximum there was 2.4).
  A staged greedy adversary (lagadv2.cpp; leftmost tie rule) on n consecutive vertices, after 8
  sweeps 0..n-1, reaches the lags listed in REPORT.md: they grow by about 0.3 per doubling of n.
  Mechanism: a vertex of top lag is fired at once unless another ready vertex ties it, so the
  adversary raises two vertices to a new level with one add (both neighbours of the added vertex),
  loses one to the firing, and repeats on the survivors: each +1/5 of lag costs a constant factor
  in the number of vertices.  This is consistent with Lemma 2 (lag at y needs chips away from y)
  and does not affect Theorem A: in all these runs height - t/5 <= 0.8.
  Not proved: that the growth continues for ever (no explicit infinite construction is given).
