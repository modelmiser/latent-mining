# Task: prove a constant bound (try), for a firing strategy in a chip game

Background. Folder 25-throttled-chips/ (READ-ONLY for you) contains a game (definition: chain/3-question.txt; overview: README.md). Chips are added one per round at integer points from a list fixed in advance; the Firer may fire at most one ready point per round (a point with at least 2 chips sends one chip to each neighbour). solver-a/proofs.txt THEOREM 2 gives a Firer strategy: with k_x = number of chips added at x so far, target T_x = (3 k_x + k_(x-1) + k_(x+1))/5, lag l_x = T_x - F_x (F_x = firings at x so far); each round, after the add, fire a ready point of largest lag provided that lag is >= 1, else pass. The proof shows l_x <= 6.6 + sqrt(t), hence height <= t/5 + 2 sqrt(t) + 13.2, hence G(h) >= 5h - 10 sqrt(5h) - 72. In every experiment (referee's exhaustive runs on short lists and beam searches for bad lists up to length 3000; two reviewers' simulations) the lag never exceeded 2.4 and height - t/5 never exceeded 3.2. Read THEOREM 1, THEOREM 2 and the conjecture in solver-a/proofs.txt, solver-b/proofs.txt (P3 and Lemma Z), and reports/referee.md.

Goal, in order of preference:
 (A) Prove that under this strategy, or under a modified strategy of your own, the height of every point stays at most t/5 + c for an absolute constant c at every step, against every list. That gives G(h) >= 5h - 5c - O(1): the shortest winning list has length 5h minus a constant. State the theorem with an explicit c and prove it completely.
 (B) Failing (A), prove a bound with an error term smaller than sqrt(t) (for example O(log t), or O(t^(1/3))), completely.
 (C) Failing that, find out why (A) is hard: either exhibit lists on which the lag (or height - t/5) of this strategy grows without bound (give the construction and evidence), which would show the conjectured constant is false for THIS strategy, or identify precisely the step where a constant bound would need a new idea. A clearly stated partial result with a complete proof is worth more than an ambitious sketch.

Ideas you may or may not use: the height identity eta_x = eta*_x + 2 l_x - l_(x-1) - l_(x+1) with eta*_x the 5-point mean of k; the fact that a point of maximal lag M has eta_x >= eta*_x >= T_x/3 >= M/3 (so it is ready once M >= 6): perhaps a sharper readiness condition (using the discrete Laplacian of l being >= 0 at a maximum, and the structure of T) gives readiness at a much smaller lag; a potential that is not quadratic; or analysing the strategy "fire the ready point with the largest height" or "largest eta_x - eta*_x" instead. Also consider the simplest target: the upper bound side shows C(h) <= 5h - 6 and computations suggest C(h) = 5h - 10 for h >= 4 for the constant list; for the constant list alone the exact behaviour might be provable.

Use computation to guide and to test (small exhaustive searches over lists, adversarial beam searches trying to maximise lag or height - t/5 under your strategy; a lemma you rely on should be tested mechanically where possible, and you should watch a test fail on a deliberately wrong statement at least once). But the deliverable is a proof, marked COMPLETE or SKETCH honestly; a sketch presented as a proof is worse than no proof.
