# Seed

Chips sit on the vertices of a graph. A vertex holding at least as many chips as it has neighbours may fire: it sends one chip along each of its edges. On a connected graph the process either stops or goes on forever, and which of the two depends only on the starting position and not on the order of firing; on a path or a cycle the stopping positions and the number of firings are known exactly. Now let two players use the process: one adds chips one at a time where it likes, the other chooses which ready vertex fires and when, and they pursue opposite aims, for example the adder wants some vertex to hold many chips at once or wants the process to run for many firings, and the other wants the opposite. For paths, cycles and small grids: what can each side force, and how many chips does the adder need?
