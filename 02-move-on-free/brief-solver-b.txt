THE GAME

Cells are 0, 1, 2, .... Fix an integer M >= 3. Blocks have size 1, 2 or 3 and occupy contiguous cells. The heap starts empty. An adversary who sees the whole layout issues requests one at a time, forever:
 - allocate(s), s in {1,2,3}: legal only if (number of live cells) + s <= M. The manager must immediately place the new block on s contiguous free cells.
 - free(b): a live block b chosen by the adversary vanishes.

Three games on these requests:
 - N (no moves): blocks never move.
 - F (move on free): immediately after free(b) with |b| = s, and before the next request, the manager may perform a sequence of moves whose moved sizes total at most s - 1. A move lifts one live block and puts it on any contiguous cells that are free once it is lifted (so it may overlap its own old position). The cells b vacated count as free. Unused allowance is lost; nothing can be saved up. So: freeing a 1 grants nothing; freeing a 2 grants one move of a 1; freeing a 3 grants one move of a 2, or up to two moves of 1s (possibly the same 1 twice). Blocks of size 3 never move. No moves at any other time.
 - O (only 1s freed): game N, except the adversary may free only blocks of size 1.

For G in {N, F, O}, h^G(M) = the least H such that some manager can serve every legal infinite request sequence using only cells 0 ... H-1. So h^O(M) <= h^F(M) <= h^N(M).

METHOD NOTES (suggestions, not requirements)
 - For fixed G, M, H this is a finite safety game. A position is a layout of cells 0..H-1: each cell free or part of a block of size 1, 2 or 3, with at most M live cells. Block identities do not matter. Compute the greatest fixpoint of safe positions: safe iff every legal allocate has a placement leading to a safe position and every legal free (after the manager's best permitted moves, in F) leads to a safe position.
 - Reflection of the line (cell i <-> H-1-i) is a symmetry you may use, but get a correct plain version first.
 - Sanity checks that would expose a wrong model: h^O <= h^F <= h^N; each h is non-decreasing in M and at least M; h^N for sizes restricted to {1} must equal M, and for sizes {1,2} check against a hand analysis of small M; a claimed manager strategy must survive exhaustive play by every adversary.
