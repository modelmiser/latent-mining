# Review brief: latent-mining experiment 17 (AND with cards, one cut and a peek)

Repository folder under review: `17-cut-and-peek/` (present in the working tree; it is not committed yet). Read the files, not this summary. Assume the authors (instances of one AI model) are competent and wrong until you have traced each claim yourself. The protocol is defined in `17-cut-and-peek/chain/3-question.txt` (formal) and `README.md` ("The protocol").

The most important claim is C1. It may answer a question that published papers list as open, so a wrong proof here would be costly. Spend most of your effort on Q1-Q3. Do the algebra yourself; do not accept a step because it looks standard.

Claims made in `17-cut-and-peek/README.md`:

- C1. (T2) For n >= 3 and R = Z_m there is no valid protocol for any m. Proof in `solver-b/proofs.txt` T2 and written out in the README section "Why full opening fails for three players".
- C2. The README's generalisation: for any three players and any fixing of the rest, if seven of the eight rows are rotations of one word then so is the eighth, whichever corner is the odd one; and its consequence for n-bit equality, n >= 4.
- C3. r(3) <= 36 by the explicit protocol `solver-a/w_n3_m36.txt`.
- C4. No valid three-player protocol with m <= 15 (searches), and the proved constraints T1, T4, T5, C3 of `solver-b/proofs.txt`; L4, L5' of `solver-a/proofs.txt`.
- C5. The README's status wording and its "What to distrust" section match what the files support.

Questions. Give a verdict for each: CONFIRMED / REFUTED / UNSUPPORTED, with file:line evidence.

- Q1. C1, set-up. Is it true that with R = Z_m privacy forces the seven AND-0 rows (for three chosen players, others fixed to 1) to be rotations of one word, and that if the all-ones row is also a rotation then correctness fails? Is row(x) = W - 2(x1 a1 + x2 a2 + x3 a3) with a_i supported on disjoint sets right, including positions where a player's two words agree and house cards? Does the argument need m, the alphabet, or the partition to satisfy anything else?
- Q2. C1, algebra. Check (*) and the formula for row(1,1,1). Check the Fourier step: at a frequency r where the transform of W is non-zero, omega^(theta_i) + omega^(theta_k) = 1 + omega^(r s_ik). Check the case split: is "two unit complex numbers with a given non-zero sum are determined as a set" true; is the zero-sum case handled completely (m odd; theta_i or theta_k equal to 0; the definition of an exceptional pair; the identity r(s_ik - s_i - s_k) = -2 theta_k mod m)? Is "two pairs cannot both be exceptional" proved for every choice of two pairs, not only (1,2),(1,3)? In the case with no exceptional pair, why are at most one of the theta non-zero, and is r t = theta_1 + theta_2 + theta_3 right? In the case with one exceptional pair (k,l), is theta_i = 0 forced for the third index, and is omega^(r t) = -1 right? Does equality of transforms at every frequency (including those where W's transform vanishes) give row(1,1,1) = z^t W? Note that the shifts s_i, s_ik are not unique when W has a period: does the argument depend on the choice?
- Q3. Try to break C1 concretely. Either construct a counterexample (a word W and vectors a_i with disjoint supports such that seven rows are rotations of W and the eighth is not), or explain why small cases cannot exist. If you can run code in your head or reason about small m (say m <= 8), do so. Also consider degenerate cases: a_i = 0, W constant, W periodic.
- Q4. C2. Is the re-coordinatisation argument (replace x_i by 1 - x_i) valid, so that the lemma holds for any odd corner? Does the consequence for n-bit equality with n >= 4 follow (fix x4 = ... = 0; which inputs of the cube have all bits equal; what privacy and correctness say for a general Boolean function in the full-open single-cut setting)? Is the README right that this does NOT cover three-bit equality?
- Q5. C3. Check `solver-a/w_n3_m36.txt` against the definition as far as you can: is it a legal protocol (partition, each player non-empty, words), and does `verifier/vcheck.py` compute M(x) from the definition and test privacy on all seven AND-0 inputs and disjointness against 1,1,1? Is the README's description of the strand structure accurate?
- Q6. C4. Read T1 and T4 of `solver-b/proofs.txt` and say whether each is a proof. For the searches: is the README right about what the referee did and did not recompute, and about the shared normalisation?
- Q7. Check each Status cell and each bullet of "What to distrust" against `reports/referee.md`, `reports/solver-a.md`, `reports/solver-b.md`, `prior-art.md` and `prior-art-deep.md`. Flag any number, count or attribution that differs from its source. Does the README claim more novelty than `prior-art-deep.md` supports? Is every statement about the outside papers attributed to the instance that read them rather than asserted as verified? (You cannot see the papers; judge only consistency and hedging.)
- Q8. Is the top-level `README.md` row for experiment 17 consistent with the folder, and is the README's "The protocol" the same object as `chain/3-question.txt` (shift direction, what M(x) is, the two validity conditions)?

For each defect give severity (CRITICAL: a headline claim is false or unproved; MODERATE: a stated claim is wrong or unsupported but the headline stands; LOW: wording), file:line, and the replacement wording you would use.
