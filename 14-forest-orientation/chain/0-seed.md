# Seed

Edges arrive one at a time on a fixed set of n vertices, chosen by an adversary who sees everything, with the promise that the graph is always a forest. Each edge must be given a direction when it arrives, and at all times every vertex may have at most d edges pointing out of it. To keep that bound the algorithm may reverse edges it oriented earlier, and each reversal costs one flip. With d = 1 every tree must be oriented towards a single root, joining two trees forces one of them to be re-rooted, and always re-rooting the smaller tree costs at most about (n/2) log2 n flips in total, which an adversary can also force up to a constant factor. For each d: what is the exact worst-case total number of flips F(n, d) that the best algorithm must pay over n - 1 edge arrivals, how quickly does the cost fall as d grows, and what does a good algorithm do when it has slack: which vertices should keep their spare out-edges in reserve?
