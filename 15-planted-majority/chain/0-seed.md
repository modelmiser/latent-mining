# Seed

A population of n identical agents, with no names, each holds one of s states. A scheduler repeatedly picks an ordered pair of agents; the two change state according to one fixed rule that depends only on their two states. The population should come to agree on something about its starting configuration, for example which of two starting opinions was in the majority, and stay agreed. Under a scheduler that is merely fair, four states suffice for exact majority and three do not; the predicates computable with unboundedly many states are known. Now let the scheduler be an adversary who is restricted only mildly (for example, it may not ignore an agent for more than a bounded number of meetings). For each s: which questions about the starting configuration can still be answered, how many meetings can the adversary force before agreement, and what do the best rules look like?
