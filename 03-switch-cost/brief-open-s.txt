You are trying to prove a general theorem about a precisely defined scheduling game, alone. Earlier solvers settled small cases by computer; nobody has an argument for all P. A proof with a gap must be labelled as having a gap. Do not search the web.

THE GAME

Parameters: integers P >= 1 (maximum job length) and T >= 1 (horizon). Time is ticks 0, 1, 2, ....

 - Jobs. A job is (r, p, d) with integers 1 <= p <= P and r + p <= d <= T. It is revealed at the start of tick r and may run in tick r. Identical jobs may be released more than once (a multiset).
 - Machine (switch cost 1). In each tick it does exactly one of: run one unit of one released, unfinished job; idle; spend a switch tick. Let A be the job most recently run (none at the start). Running a job B != A while A is unfinished requires the tick immediately before B's unit to be a switch tick. If A is finished, or nothing has run yet, any job may be started or resumed with no switch tick. Idling leaves A unchanged. A switch tick is not aimed at a particular job: after it, the next tick may run any job. Running A itself never needs a switch tick.
 - Order of play at tick t: the adversary, who has seen the online schedule for ticks 0..t-1, releases any finite multiset of jobs with r = t (possibly none); then the online scheduler, seeing them, chooses what the machine does in tick t.
 - Promise. After every release, the multiset of ALL jobs released so far must be feasible for some hindsight schedule built from tick 0 that respects release times and completes every job by its deadline. Two versions of the game differ only in which machine the hindsight schedule runs on:
     same-cost promise: the machine above (switch cost 1);
     free promise: the same machine with switch cost 0, i.e. ordinary preemptive feasibility.
   The hindsight schedule has nothing to do with what the online scheduler actually did.
 - Value. The online scheduler always pays switch cost 1 and keeps working after tick T until every job is done (no job is released with r >= T). Completion of a job = index of the tick after its last unit. Lateness = max(0, completion - d). The online scheduler wants to minimise the maximum lateness over all jobs; the adversary wants to maximise it.
   S_T(P) = value of the game under the same-cost promise; F_T(P) = value under the free promise. S(P), F(P) = limits as T grows (both sequences are nondecreasing in T).

WHAT IS KNOWN (from two independent solvers; a referee is checking it in parallel, so treat it as probable, not certain)

The idea under the same-cost promise: open with a slack "decoy" job whose deadline is T; whatever the scheduler does with it, the hindsight schedule is the one that did the opposite; then keep two incompatible hindsight plans alive.

YOUR GOAL
Prove S(P) = P - 1 for every P >= 2: give an explicit adversary, valid under the SAME-COST promise, that forces lateness P - 1 on some job against every online scheduler, and say what horizon T it needs (is it about P^2?). This is the harder promise: the hindsight schedule must itself pay a switch tick whenever it leaves an unfinished job, so the adversary must always be able to exhibit a hindsight schedule that orders the jobs well. Work on the same-cost promise; a proof for it also proves the free-promise case, since S <= F.
