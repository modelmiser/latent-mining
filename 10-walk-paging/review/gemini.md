### Important items not asked about (most severe first)

1. **Checker blind spots for zero-fault infinite cycles:** Both solvers' standalone checkers (`solver-b/verify.py` and `solver-a/adv.py`) had structural blind spots that would have accepted infinite loops without faults. Correctness of the adversaries against non-termination was established only because the referee's independent C++ program (`verifier/wk.cpp:211-236`) explicitly tracked zero-fault cycles and confirmed 0 occurrences across all reachable states for k ≤ 13, m ≤ 6.
2. **Triviality of Solver A's witness plays:** `solver-a/witnesses.txt` contains exclusively straight-line walks of length 2m, failing to demonstrate the non-trivial mechanics (frontier bounces, block transitions, or reversals) of Theorem L. Solver B's `witness_plays.txt` correctly provides non-straight witness walks.
3. **Model sensitivity to two-sided prefetching:** The game model restricts prefetching on fault to exactly one neighbor \(q \in \{p-1, p+1\}\). As flagged in `reports/referee.md:65`, allowing two-sided prefetch (\(C' \subseteq C \cup \{p-1, p, p+1\}\)) alters the offline-to-online entry ratios and remains open.
