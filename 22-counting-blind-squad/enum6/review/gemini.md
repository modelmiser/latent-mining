### Important Points Not Asked About

1. **Asymmetric Verification of Rejections (False-Negative Risk)**: The second independent simulator (`enum6/check.py`) only re-simulated the 2,502 rules scoring \(N \ge 7\) (`enum6/check_s6_ge7.txt`). None of the 78,364,161,594 rejected rules were independently rechecked. If `simulate` in `enum.c` had a bug causing premature failure (such as false cycle reporting or state packing collision), an optimal rule could have been discarded silently without `check.py` catching it. The conclusion rests on the independent backtracking counts in `reports/referee.md`.
2. **Discovery of Unreported Second Optimal Family**: The 336 optimal rules split into two distinct firing-time profiles: 312 rules firing at \([7, 9, 9, 15, 17, 13, 13]\) (52 orbits of size 6 under state permutations) and 24 rules firing at \([8, 10, 10, 19, 21, 20, 20]\) (4 orbits of size 6). Prior solver and referee reports only recorded a single witness (`solver-b/witness_s6.txt`) belonging to the 312-rule family; the full enumeration is the first to identify and catalog the 24-rule family.
3. **Non-Monotonic Firing Times**: Both optimal families fire lines of lengths 7 and 8 strictly earlier than length 6 (\(t(6)=17 > t(7)=13\) and \(t(6)=21 > t(7)=20\)), confirming that firing time does not scale monotonically with line length under counting-blind rules.
