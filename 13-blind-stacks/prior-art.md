# Prior-art search: blind sorting with k capacity-2 stacks in series

Setting, in standard terms: permutations sortable by bounded-capacity stacks in series (permutation classes, token passing networks), with the twist that the sorter sees no values and learns only from refused output attempts (an incomplete-information / decision-tree game).

## CLOSEST PRIOR WORK

1. **Permutations generated by token passing in graphs** — Atkinson, Livesey, Tulley, 1997. https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/permutations-generated-by-token-passing-in-graphs/ Shows that permutations produced by a finite network of bounded-capacity nodes are described by regular sets, so they can be counted by length. Differs: the sorter has full information; it counts achievable permutations, not orders beaten by one blind strategy.
2. **Permutations generated by a stack of depth 2 and an infinite stack in series** — Elder, 2005/2006. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0510244 Gives a 20-element basis for that class. Differs: full information, second stack unbounded.
3. **Methods of computing deque sortable permutations given complete and incomplete information** — Denton, 2012. https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.1532 Shows some deque-sortable permutations cannot reliably be sorted when each element is revealed only when pushed. Differs: values become visible once drawn; deque, not capacity-2 stacks in series; no count of orders per strategy seen.
4. **OEIS A052936**, expansion of (1-x)(1-2x)/(1-5x+5x^2). https://oeis.org/A052936 Lists 1, 2, 7, 25, 90, 325, 1175, ... with recurrence a(n)=5a(n-1)-5a(n-2) and Fibonacci/Lucas closed forms (Greubel, 2019). Differs: no sorting interpretation on the page (only "generalized compositions"); N(2,n) = 3·A052936(n-2) for n≥3 is my own term-by-term comparison.
5. **Permutations sortable by two stacks in series** — Elvey Price, Guttmann, 2015. https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.08663 Exact counts below length 20 and numerical asymptotics for unbounded stacks. Differs: unbounded, full information.
6. **Sorting with a forklift** — Albert, Atkinson, 2002. https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0209016 Sorting with one generalised stack moving several items at once, including bounded moves. Differs: single device, full information.

## VERDICTS

- **Headline, N(2,n) = 5N(2,n-1) − 5N(2,n-2), n≥5; generating function; Fibonacci/Lucas form: NOT FOUND** as a sorting result. The number sequence is three times a shifted A052936, and the reports' Fibonacci/Lucas closed form agrees with the formula on that page, so the algebra is known; the combinatorial meaning is not recorded there.
- **N(1,n) = 2^(n−1): LIKELY EASY COROLLARY.** Search snippets credit Atkinson–Livesey–Tulley with rational enumeration of bounded single stacks; I did not see the value 2^(n−1) stated in a source, so the link is my inference.
- **N(k,n) = n! iff n ≤ k+1: NOT FOUND.**
- **N(k,k+2) = (k+2)! − (k+1) (conjecture): NOT FOUND.**
- **k=3 and k=4 recurrences (conjectures): NOT FOUND.**
- **The blind-feedback model itself: NOT FOUND.** Nearest are Denton and permutation-guessing games (Mastermind variants).
