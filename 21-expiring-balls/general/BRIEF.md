# Task: general bounds for the expiring-balls game (experiment 21)

Known: N(1..4) = 2, 4, 7, 12 (N(k) = least number of bins with which the Adversary forces load k against first-in-first-out expiry); N(k) <= N(k-1) + 2^(k-1); without expiry the answer is 2^k; if the Adversary may remove any ball, 1 + k(k+1)/2 bins suffice. The fit k + 2^(k-1) matches the four values and is unproved. Nothing is proved about N(k) from below for general k beyond what the small cases give.
Goals, with complete proofs: (1) a lower bound on N(k) for all k (how many bins does the Adversary NEED under FIFO expiry?): for example N(k) >= c·2^k for some c, or N(k) >= N(k-1) + something growing, or at least superpolynomial growth; equivalently a strategy for the Rule on few bins, proved safe. (3) Or N(5) bounds by computation with checkable evidence (the page gives N(5) <= 28 from the recursion; any explicit Adversary strategy on fewer bins, verified by a checker written from the definition, or any proved lower bound above the trivial one).
